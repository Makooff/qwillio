# Prose Clean

Remove the tells that make writing read as AI-generated. Plain, direct, specific. If a sentence survives deletion of a word without losing meaning, delete the word.

## Banned openers & connectors

Cut on sight: *dive into, delve, unleash, unlock, harness, leverage, elevate, empower, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-evolving landscape, it's worth noting that, it's important to note, moreover, furthermore, additionally, that being said, at the end of the day, when it comes to, navigate the complexities of.*

## Structural tells

- **Rule of three everywhere** — "fast, reliable, and scalable" on every line. Vary. Sometimes one adjective. Sometimes none.
- **Gratuitous em-dashes** — replace most with a period or comma. An em-dash should be rare and deliberate.
- **Hedging stacks** — "may potentially sometimes" → pick one or cut.
- **Signposting overload** — "First… Second… Finally… In conclusion". Let structure carry itself.
- **Empty summaries** — a closing paragraph that restates what was just said. Cut it.
- **Vague intensifiers** — very, really, quite, extremely, incredibly. Usually deletable.
- **"Not only… but also"** — almost always rewritable to something shorter.
- **Title Case Headings For Everything** — use sentence case.

## Rewrite patterns

| Slop | Human |
| --- | --- |
| "Let's dive into how to…" | "Here's how to…" |
| "leverage the power of X" | "use X" |
| "In today's fast-paced world, users expect…" | "Users expect…" |
| "This robust, scalable, and seamless solution" | "This handles X without Y" |
| "It's worth noting that the API is fast" | "The API is fast" |
| "helps you to easily and quickly…" | "…" (show it, don't claim it) |

## Method

1. Delete the first sentence if it's throat-clearing. The real point is usually sentence two.
2. Replace abstract claims with concrete specifics (numbers, names, examples).
3. Read it aloud. Anything you wouldn't say to a colleague, cut or rewrite.
4. Prefer short words: *use* not *utilize*, *help* not *facilitate*, *about* not *regarding*.
5. One idea per sentence. Break long sentences at the "and" or ";".
6. End when the point is made. No wrap-up paragraph.

## Keep

Voice, specificity, and honest enthusiasm. Cleaning ≠ flattening. A human wrote a real thing — keep the real thing, drop the filler around it.
